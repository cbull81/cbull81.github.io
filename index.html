About

Hello! I am a current M.S. student in the Computational Epidemiology and Systems Modeling program at the University of Michigan. I graduated with a bachelor's degree in Economics and in Mathematical Methods and the Social Sciences (MMSS) from Northwestern University. After working as a data scientist in healthcare consulting for a few years, I returned to graduate school to focus on the intersection of mathematical modeling and public health.

Coming from a a background in the quantitative social sciences, I am enthusiastic about interdisciplinary approaches to epidemiologic research. I aim to apply a complex systems approach to the study of infectious diseases and climate change, with the understanding that evolving biological, environmental, and social dynamics must be understood holistically to address the emergent health threats of the Anthropocene.

Research Interests

  • Infectious Disease Modeling
  • Complex Systems
  • Climate Change and Health
  • Machine Learning
  • Urban and Spatial Epidemiology
  • Human Ecology

I currently work as a research assistant for Prof. Jennifer Head's Climate Sensitive Infections research group. We leverage a host of modeling approaches to understand the impact of extreme weather on infectious pathogens like Blastomycosis and Coccidiomycosis. Much of my work involves analzying a national sample of Medicare claims from the Centers for Medicare and Medicaid (CMS) to evaluate the acute effects of extreme weather events on infectious disease hospitalization.

As a second-year masters' student, I am also working on a capstone project with Prof. Joe Eisenberg to study Dengue transmission dynamics in a Northwestern Ecuador. Specifically, I am focusing on building a network metapopulation model for human movement between villages surrounding a system of river basins.
